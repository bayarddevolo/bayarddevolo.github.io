Nicaragua

Research, books, and articles on gender, revolution, and social movements in Nicaragua, with a focus on the Sandinista period and its aftermath.

Overview

My engagement with Nicaragua spans [X] years of fieldwork, archival research, and interviews with political actors and movement participants. My work examines how revolutionary politics shaped — and were shaped by — gender identity, maternal symbolism, and women's political mobilization during and after the 1979 Sandinista revolution.

This research draws on ethnographic fieldwork in Nicaragua, oral histories with mothers of fallen combatants, and analysis of state and movement archives. It sits at the intersection of feminist political sociology, Latin American studies, and the anthropology of revolution.

Book

[ Book cover

Mothers of Heroes and Martyrs: Gender Identity Politics in Nicaragua, 1979–1999

Johns Hopkins University Press, 2001

This book examines how Nicaraguan mothers of fallen Sandinista combatants were mobilized politically by the revolutionary state, and how they negotiated, contested, and transformed the maternal imagery deployed in their name. Drawing on extensive fieldwork and interviews, it traces the rise and decline of the Mothers of Heroes and Martyrs movement across two decades of revolutionary and post-revolutionary politics.

The study reveals how gender identity — particularly idealized motherhood — served as a central resource for political mobilization, and how women activists both embraced and challenged these representations in pursuit of their own agendas.

Research Themes

Maternal Symbolism and Revolutionary Politics

How did the Sandinista state deploy images of grieving and heroic motherhood to consolidate political support? And how did real mothers — organized into a national movement — respond to, inhabit, and resist these representations?

Women's Political Mobilization

The Sandinista revolution produced significant openings for women's political participation, yet also reproduced gendered hierarchies within the revolutionary movement. This tension between liberation and constraint runs through much of my Nicaraguan work.

Post-Revolutionary Gender Politics

Following the 1990 electoral defeat of the FSLN, my research tracks how women's organizations and the mothers' movement adapted to — and shaped — the post-revolutionary political landscape across the 1990s.

Methodology: Feminist Fieldwork and Oral History

My Nicaragua research is grounded in long-term ethnographic fieldwork, oral histories, and archival sources. I reflect throughout on the politics of research relationships and the methodological challenges of studying politically charged communities.
